HCDP Election Protection & Policy Report

Poll Worker Policy & Reform Report

Addressing the 400-worker shortage in Hidalgo County by identifying administrative bottlenecks, proposing flexible split shifts (4-hr, 6-hr, 8-hr), streamlining poll worker training, and requesting official action from the Elections Administrator.

Executive Summary • Priority Issue

Why Is Hidalgo County Short 400 Poll Workers?

Despite recent pay increases approved by the county ($19/hr for Election Judges, $17/hr for Alternate Judges, and $15/hr for Clerks), the Hidalgo County Democratic Party and grassroots organizers have compiled widespread feedback explaining why polling locations remain severely understaffed for the November 3, 2026 General Election.

Reform #1 • Shift Flexibility

1. Exhausting 15+ Hour Shift Marathons vs. Flexible Split Shifts

❌ Current Problem: The 15-Hour Barrier

Currently, applicants are mandated to work a continuous shift from 5:45 a.m. to 9:45 p.m.+. This single requirement disqualifies working parents, caregivers, students with class schedules, and elderly residents who cannot endure 16 consecutive hours on their feet.

✅ Proposed Solution: 4-Hour, 6-Hour & 8-Hour Split Shifts

Request the Hidalgo County Elections Administrator establish option for split shifts (e.g. Morning 5:45 AM–12:00 PM, Afternoon 12:00 PM–5:00 PM, Evening 4:30 PM–CLOSE). Splitting shifts immediately unlocks hundreds of willing applicants.

📜 Texas Election Code § 32.072 (Selectivity of Shift Lengths):
"The authority conducting an election may authorize election clerks to work less than the total number of hours the polling place is open. An election clerk authorized to work part-time may begin work after the polls open or leave before the polls close."
Reform #2 • Onboarding Modernization

2. Streamlining the Cumbersome Poll Worker Training Process

❌ Current Problem: Cumbersome Portal Setup

Applicants report that navigating the online poll worker training portal requires complex multi-step self-registration, creating confusion and causing high drop-off rates before training completion.

✅ Proposed Solution: 1-Click Registration & Hybrid Support

Provide streamlined direct links, step-by-step video guides, and hybrid in-person training sessions at local precinct centers. HCDP will offer a Poll Worker Concierge Desk to walk applicants through registration.

Formal Administrative Requests

Formal Recommendations for the Hidalgo County Elections Administrator

1

Authorize Split Shifts for November 3, 2026 General Election

Utilize statutory authority under TX Election Code § 32.072 to offer 4-hour, 6-hour, and 8-hour split shift options for Election Clerks across all county polling locations.

2

Publish Simplified Onboarding & Training Manuals

Issue a streamlined, single-page training registration guide in English and Spanish to reduce digital onboarding drop-off rates.

3

Establish Quarterly Poll Worker Advisory Council

Create a formal advisory body comprised of experienced election judges, precinct chairs, and poll clerks to regularly review administrative updates, equipment feedback, and compensation timelines.

County Records & Legal References

Hidalgo County Election Board Records & Legislative Context

A review of recent Hidalgo County Commissioners Court & Election Board proceedings confirms:

  • Hourly Rate Adjustment: The County Commissioners Court approved increased hourly rates ($19/hr Judge, $17/hr Alt Judge, $15/hr Clerk) to address recruiting deficits.
  • Mandatory Training Mandate: Texas Secretary of State rules require certified training completion prior to each election cycle.
  • Statutory Authority for Split Shifts: Texas Election Code § 32.072 provides full legal authority to implement part-time clerk positions without requiring additional legislative action.

Formal Policy Letter & Email Template

✉️ Formal Letter & Communication to Hidalgo County Elections Administrator

Below is the formal administrative request and collaborative partnership proposal prepared for submission to the Hidalgo County Elections Administrator (elections@co.hidalgo.tx.us). Precinct chairs, election advocates, and community members can copy or send this letter directly.

To: Hidalgo County Elections Administrator <elections@co.hidalgo.tx.us>
Cc: Hidalgo County Democratic Party Executive Committee <info@hidalgocountydems.org>
Subject: Formal Partnership Proposal & Administrative Recommendations: Poll Worker Recruitment, Split Shifts (§ 32.072), and Onboarding Optimization for Nov 3, 2026
Dear Elections Administrator & Department Officers, On behalf of election workers, precinct chairs, and community voting rights advocates across Hidalgo County, we appreciate your office’s dedication to managing free, fair, and accessible elections. As we prepare for the November 3, 2026 General Election, we recognize the vital mission facing your department to staff polling places across all county precincts. Based on feedback from veteran election judges and prospective applicants, we wish to submit formal recommendations to address the current 400-poll worker deficit and offer our organizational resources to support your recruitment drive: 1. Authorization of Flexible Split Shifts (Texas Election Code § 32.072): Mandating 14- to 16-hour continuous shifts (5:45 AM to 9:45 PM+) remains the single largest barrier preventing working parents, caregivers, students, and seniors from serving. Under TX Election Code § 32.072 & § 32.073, your office holds statutory authority to authorize split shifts (e.g., 4-hour, 6-hour, or 8-hour shifts) for election clerks. Authorizing split shifts will immediately unlock hundreds of available applicants. 2. Modernization of the Online Training & Onboarding Portal: Applicants report high drop-off rates due to multi-step portal registration friction. We recommend issuing single-page onboarding guides and offering hybrid in-person training. To support your staff, our community coalition offers to host voluntary Applicant Concierge Desks to walk applicants through registration. 3. High School & College Student Clerk Program (TX Election Code § 32.0511): Expanding student election clerk recruitment with UTRGV, STC, and high schools provides young residents with hands-on civic leadership while filling clerk positions. 4. Transparent Stipend Payment Schedules: Providing clear written payout timelines upon training completion builds trust and boosts worker retention. Our Party's Offer of Direct Support: The Hidalgo County Democratic Party is prepared to mobilize our 257 precinct chairs, digital platforms, and volunteer network to co-recruit and fill the 400-worker gap. We request a meeting to discuss these recommendations and partner on recruitment. Thank you for your service to Hidalgo County voters. Respectfully submitted, Hidalgo County Democratic Party & Election Protection Coalition Email: info@hidalgocountydems.org | Phone: (956) 212-0476
